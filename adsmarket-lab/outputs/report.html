1 · Marketplace experiments: interference through shared budgets

About half of advertisers exhaust their daily budget. When queries are randomized, the treatment arm's higher prices drain budgets the control arm would otherwise have spent, so control looks worse than a real all-control world. The query-split test reads +9.5% revenue; the true global effect is +4.1%. A budget-split design (each advertiser's budget halved across two isolated mini-markets) reads +4.1% (95% CI +1.4% to +6.8%).

Decision: any test that touches pricing, ranking or pacing in budget-constrained segments must run as budget-split, or the experimentation platform will systematically over-ship.

2 · Reserve-price optimization with marketplace-health guardrails

Revenue-maximizing is not the same as marketplace-optimal. Under guardrails of ≤8% advertiser-surplus loss and ≤5% fill-rate loss, the recommended reserve is $0.25: revenue +2.1%, advertiser surplus -5.1%, incremental installs -4.7%. Going all the way to the revenue peak ($0.60) adds only 1.8 more points of revenue and costs 14.3 more points of advertiser surplus. That surplus loss is a churn risk the short-run revenue number doesn't show.

3 · Incrementality from observational auction logs

Comparing won vs lost auctions overstates the effect of winning 4.4×, because advertisers bid up returning, high-intent users who install anyway. Even full-sample AIPW misses. Overlap diagnostics show why: owners almost never lose auctions on their own brand keyword, so positivity fails and nearly all the confounding sits in brand auctions. The observational estimand is therefore restricted to non-brand auctions with common support. There, cross-fitted AIPW estimates 0.0488 against a truth of 0.0504, and its 95% CI covers the truth (it also covered in 5 of 5 independently simulated worlds). Brand incrementality is handed to the ghost-ads experiment in section 4, the design that can actually identify it.
